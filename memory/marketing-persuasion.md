# Marketing & persuasion levers — for copy, funnels, and offers

Captured for future marketing use (from a creator reel, "things people do to manipulate you," 2026-07).
These are **influence mechanics**. Execute uses them **only to communicate genuine value more
persuasively** — never to deceive, coerce, or manufacture false urgency. Each entry says how it works
and the **honest** way to use it (or why to avoid it). Several of these are manipulation, not
persuasion — flagged **DEFEND, don't use**: recognize them (in others' pitches, in a client's
competitors) but keep them out of Execute's own copy.

## Legit persuasion levers (use in honest marketing)
- **Anchoring** — set a high reference point first so the real price reads as small. *Honest use:* anchor
  against the true cost of the alternative (e.g. "a bootcamp is $15k; this is $X"), not a fake inflated
  number. Real comparison, not invented.
- **Future pacing** — the brain feels imagined outcomes almost like real ones; make the reader vividly
  picture the specific after-state your product delivers, so they feel the payoff. *Honest use:* paint an
  outcome the product can actually deliver. Aspiration, not a lie.
- **Commitment escalation (foot-in-the-door)** — a big ask ("run a marathon") gets refused; a small first
  step ("run a 5k") that grows does not. *Honest use:* a low-friction first action (free tool, 1-line
  signup, tiny win) then a natural next step. This is just good onboarding — Execute already designs
  onboarding as a fast-aha funnel.
- **Emotional mirroring (empathy)** — reflect the reader's actual feeling ("this is exhausting, I know")
  so they feel understood and trust you. *Honest use:* genuinely name the pain you solve; empathy that's
  real, not performed to extract.
- **Authority / social proof (the honest side of "triangulation")** — a claim lands harder from a credible
  source than from you. *Honest use:* real testimonials, real names, real results, real credentials —
  never fabricated endorsements. (The reel's "Jeff Bezos said…" version is fine only if he actually did.)
- **Specific concrete over vague** — "finishing your sentences" works because it signals the expert *gets*
  your exact problem. *Honest use:* copy that names the reader's exact situation earns trust; it proves you
  understand the problem, which is the real thing being sold.

## DEFEND, don't use (manipulation — recognize, never deploy)
- **Motte & Bailey** — make a strong/controversial claim, then retreat to a safe defensible one when
  challenged (and quietly keep the strong one). Bad-faith equivocation. Spot it in others' arguments.
- **Objection looping** — turn someone's objection back on them repeatedly until they're too worn down to
  resist and agree from exhaustion, not conviction. Coercion. Never in Execute's funnels — a sale made by
  fatigue churns and refunds.
- **Silence as punishment** — go cold/withhold connection to condition someone to stop a behavior. That's
  relational coercion, not marketing.

## How Execute uses this
When writing any marketing copy, landing page, or funnel: lead with the reader's real pain (mirroring +
specificity), make the genuine outcome vivid (future pacing), anchor price against the true alternative,
and design a low-friction first step that escalates naturally. Back every claim with real proof (RECEIPTS
lens). Never fabricate urgency, endorsements, or numbers — that's the manipulation line, and it also just
loses trust and converts worse long-term. Pairs with the distribution playbook in `execute.md` and the
`owner-design-taste` law for the surface it ships on.
